# Tough questions, straight answers

Generated by `make_summary.py` from the result files, with the same numbers as the one-page summary ([`ThirstMap_summary.pdf`](ThirstMap_summary.pdf)). Raw replies for every number are in `results/`.

### 1. Why synthetic landscapes? Real imagery is what matters.

Because a benchmark is only as good as its answer key. Real photos come with no ground-truth flow paths, and a person tracing streams by eye is doing exactly the skill under test. ThirstMap simulates the water, so every answer is computed, then re-checked independently. Synthetic scenes also make **twins** possible: the same landscape with one minimal change, which no photo archive can supply.

Realism is covered three ways: real terrain for 4 Indian cities from Copernicus DEM and ESA WorldCover (1,119 tasks); a realism check in which simulated drainage lies near real mapped water 1.3× to 4.2× as often as chance ([`REALISM.md`](REALISM.md)); and three image styles plus monsoon, haze and coarse-sensor looks whose answers must not change.

### 2. How do you know the answers are right?

- **Independent re-derivation.** An independent hydrology library (pysheds) recomputes the flow. It agreed on every question in 296 of 308 scenes; questions where it disagreed were dropped, never edited.

- **Evidence in the pixels.** The path must be visible in the rendered image, and every option label readable and clear of the legend, scale bar and north arrow.

- **Byte-for-byte rebuilds.** 1,398 of 1,398 audited images regenerate identically from their seeds; the exact evaluated inputs are published with SHA-256 checksums.

- **A self-audit.** Drawing model answers on the images they saw exposed two generator problems. 431 tasks (7.5%) are excluded from every score, listed in `dataset/errata.jsonl` and explained in [`AUDIT.md`](AUDIT.md). We publish our mistakes rather than hide them.

- **People.** The same questions are open to anyone at https://yugesh1111.github.io/THIRSTMAP/human/.

### 3. Aren't the samples small?

They are sized to what is free: Gemini's free tier allows about 20 requests per model per day, so the frontier check is 75 questions on 18 images. Small samples become meaningful with exact statistics: every score is tested against the exact chance of guessing each question. Gemini 3.5 Flash scores 76% against 14% chance (p = 4e-33); the open models in the main run answered 130 questions each.

On before-and-after pairs, precisely: Gemini 3.5 Flash got both halves of 3 of 7 right; Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite, Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct, Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct got 0 of 7; 0 of 26 for every open model in the main run. With only 7 pairs this is a coarse measure, and we say so: Gemini 3.5 Flash gets 71% of the pair halves right, so about 51% of pairs would be right if it answered the halves independently, close to the 43% observed. What the pairs show directly is habit: Gemini 3.5 Flash gave the same answer to the original and its twin in 29% of pairs, although the right answer had changed. The full dataset has 740 pairs for anyone with a larger budget.

### 4. Could a model score by guessing, or from the question text alone?

- **Chance is computed exactly** per question (a random guess on a 'which villages' question is right 2% of the time).

- **No-image control.** The same questions are asked without the image. In the main run the open models score within a few points of each other with and without it (SmolVLM-256M-Instruct 8% with, 10% without, SmolVLM2-2.2B-Instruct 8% with, 12% without, Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct 9% with, 9% without, Qwen2.5-VL-3B-Instruct 17% with, 14% without): they barely use the image. The Gemini models, by contrast, fall to about chance without the image (Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite 49% → 13%, Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite 40% → 15%; chance 14%).

- **Balanced answers.** Correct options are spread evenly (the wetland question's right answer is A, B, C or D 146, 142, 162, 164 times).

- **Traps.** Wrong answers that a shortcut produces are tagged; the Gemini models fall for one 8%–20% of the time.

### 5. Are the questions even answerable from the image?

Every kept question passed the pixel checks, and every image has a reveal that draws the true path. The strongest model we could run, Gemini 3.5 Flash, answers 76% of the frontier questions right (57/75): hard, but answerable.

### 6. Did you pick questions to make models fail?

No. Questions are dropped only by automated validity checks, before any model sees them. The frontier set is chosen by a seeded round-robin over twin kinds (`frontier.py select`) before any model runs, and every model gets identical prompts and images. The audit removed tasks where a hidden label could have failed a model unfairly, which raised model scores.

### 7. Why not the largest models?

Budget: this was built without paid API credit. The harness already supports `claude:`, `openai:` (any OpenAI-compatible endpoint), `gemini:` and any Hugging Face vision model; adding one is a single command, and [`LEADERBOARD.md`](../LEADERBOARD.md) rebuilds from the raw replies. We report only what we ran.

### 8. Did fine-tuning teach the skill, or just the answer format?

Both, and we separate them. The base model almost never replied in the requested format (1% well-formed) while the tuned model nearly always did (99%). Format alone does not explain the gain: without the image the tuned model scores 11%, close to chance (14%); with it 19% (paired McNemar test against the base model, p = 0.01). The tuning used only scenes that never appear in the test set.

### 9. Why does this matter beyond a benchmark?

India's data centres use about 150 billion litres of water a year, and about 70% of capacity sits in water-stressed Mumbai and Chennai. AI geospatial agents are being built to answer siting and pollution questions from imagery. A model that names the village nearest the data centre, instead of following the stream, sends the warning to the wrong village. [`POLICY_NOTE.md`](POLICY_NOTE.md) proposes a pass bar, including before-and-after pairs, before AI is used for water screening.

### 10. What does it cost to run?

- Building the full 5,761-task dataset takes about 18 minutes on 4 CPU cores; one scene with its twin takes under a minute.

- Frontier models: every question about an image goes in one request at 768 px, about 300 tokens per answer; the whole frontier check is 18 requests per model, inside a free daily quota.

- Open models run on a CPU; no GPU is needed.

### 11. Can I reproduce it?

`./run_all.sh --quick` (about 2 minutes) or `./run_all.sh` for everything; `pip install` gives a `thirstmap` command; the [Colab notebook](https://colab.research.google.com/github/yugesh1111/THIRSTMAP/blob/main/ThirstMap_quickstart.ipynb) builds a scene and scores it in the browser; `verify_results.py` re-derives every number from the raw replies.
